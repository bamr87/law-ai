## Fill every bubble

There is no penalty for a wrong answer. A blank and a wrong answer score identically, so a blank is a guess you declined to make — strictly worse. See [[article:lsat-scoring-and-percentiles]].

Two practical rules follow:

- **Reserve the last 30 seconds of every section** to fill any remaining bubbles. Set it as a checkpoint, not an intention.
- **Pick one letter and use it for all blind guesses.** Answer keys are close to evenly distributed, so a consistent letter is as good as any other choice — and it removes a decision at the moment you least want one.

## Triage is a skill, not an admission

Deciding which questions to abandon is a scored skill. The exam contains, in every section, a small number of questions that will cost you three minutes and that you will probably still miss. Trading those for three questions you would get right is straightforwardly correct, and the only reason people resist it is that skipping feels like losing.

Skip candidates, roughly in order:

- **Parallel reasoning and parallel flaw.** Six arguments to analyse. Highest time cost on the section. See [[article:lr-parallel-family]].
- **Very long stimuli** late in a section when you are behind.
- **Rule-substitution questions** in Logic Games.
- **Any question where after 45 seconds you cannot state what the answer needs to do.** That is the strongest signal available, and it is available early.

## Two kinds of skip

They are different decisions and confusing them is expensive.

**The pre-emptive skip.** You look at the question and decide before starting. Costs about five seconds. This is the good one, and it is available only if you glance at what is coming.

**The abandonment.** You have invested ninety seconds and stop. Costs the ninety seconds. Necessary sometimes, but it is a loss being cut, not a decision being made well.

Building the pre-emptive skip is what pacing practice is for. A test taker who can identify a three-minute question in five seconds has a structural advantage over one who discovers it two minutes in.

> [!example] The arithmetic of one skip
> A section leaves you four minutes for the last four questions. One is a parallel-flaw question.
>
> **No triage:** you take them in order. The parallel question eats 150 seconds and you miss it anyway. Ninety seconds remain for three questions; you rush and get one right. **Score: 1 of 4.**
>
> **With triage:** you glance ahead, guess the parallel question immediately, and spend 80 seconds each on the other three, getting all three right. The guess has a 20% chance. **Expected score: 3.2 of 4.**
>
> The gap is more than two raw points, from one decision that took five seconds.

## Guessing well when you have looked

A blind guess is 20%. An educated guess after even minimal work is much better, and the work is cheap:

- **Eliminate the extremes.** On inference questions, choices with *always*, *never*, *only* are usually wrong. See [[article:lr-inference-family]].
- **Eliminate the off-topic.** A choice introducing a term that appears nowhere in the stimulus is rarely credited.
- **On weaken and strengthen, eliminate anything that denies a premise.** See [[article:lr-strengthen-and-weaken]].

Fifteen seconds of that raises a 20% guess to 33% or 50%. Across five guessed questions that is one to one and a half raw points.

> [!trap] Guessing without flagging
> You guess, intend to return, and do not mark the question. At the end of the section you have four minutes and no idea which questions to revisit, so you spend them re-reading questions you already answered correctly.
>
> Flag every guess, always, even when you are sure you will remember. Especially then.

## Key takeaways

- No wrong-answer penalty: every bubble gets filled, with a pre-chosen letter for blind guesses.
- Reserve the last 30 seconds of each section for sweeping up blanks.
- Triage is a scored skill; the pre-emptive skip costs five seconds, the abandonment costs ninety.
- If you cannot say what the answer must do after 45 seconds, that is your signal.
- Fifteen seconds of elimination turns a 20% guess into a 33–50% one. Always flag guesses.
