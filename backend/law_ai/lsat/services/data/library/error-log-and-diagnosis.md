## Why a log beats memory

Without a record, your sense of your own weaknesses is built from the questions that annoyed you most, which is not the same as the questions that cost you most. People consistently report struggling with the families they find unpleasant rather than the ones they miss.

A log converts that into a count. After two weeks it will tell you something you did not know, and it is frequently unwelcome — the family you thought was fine turns out to be a quarter of your losses.

## What to record

One row per miss. Six fields, which fit on a phone note or a spreadsheet.

| Field | Example |
|---|---|
| Question id | `lr-041` |
| Family | Necessary assumption |
| Cause | Method — did not negate |
| What I picked and why | B; it sounded like something the author assumed |
| Why the credited answer wins | Negating D destroys the argument; negating B does not |
| Rule to carry forward | On necessary assumption, negate before comparing |

The last two fields are the ones that matter. A log of question numbers and topics is a list; a log of *rules carried forward* is a study plan.

## The four causes

Every miss has one of four causes, and they need four different responses. Assigning the cause is the single most valuable thing the log does.

- **Method.** You did not know how to attack this family. **Fix:** read the family article, drill twenty of that family untimed.
- **Execution.** You knew the method and misapplied it — negated sloppily, misread the stem, mapped the wrong paragraph. **Fix:** a specific checklist rule, applied deliberately for a week.
- **Clock.** You would have got it with another minute. **Fix:** pacing and triage, not content. See [[article:pacing-and-time-management]].
- **Reading.** You misread the stimulus, the stem, or the choice. **Fix:** slow the read, not the reasoning. Frequently this is the largest category and the most fixable.

Blind review is what makes the assignment possible, because it separates clock from method directly. See [[article:blind-review-method]].

> [!example] What two weeks of logging reveals
> A test taker believes their weakness is parallel reasoning, which they find exhausting. Two weeks of logging, 38 misses:
>
> ```
> Necessary assumption    9   (7 method, 2 execution)
> Flaw                    7   (5 reading — misread the stem, 2 method)
> Parallel                4   (3 clock, 1 method)
> RC inference            8   (6 reading, 2 method)
> Strengthen/weaken       5   (4 execution — attacked a premise)
> Other                   5
> ```
>
> Parallel reasoning is fourth, and three of its four misses are clock — meaning triage would fix them without any content work. The real story is that 13 of 38 misses are *reading* errors, split between misread flaw stems and RC inference.
>
> The prescription that falls out: a week on necessary assumption method, and a deliberate habit of re-reading the stem before the choices. Neither was what this person would have guessed.

## Reviewing the log

Weekly, ten minutes. Three questions:

1. **Which family has the most misses?** That is next week's drilling.
2. **Which cause dominates?** Method means content work; reading means a slow-down rule; clock means pacing; execution means a checklist.
3. **Are last week's carried-forward rules working?** If the same rule appears three weeks running, it is not being applied — make it a pre-question ritual instead of an intention.

That third question is where logs usually fail. A rule written and never applied is a record of a good intention, and the fix is to attach it to a moment: "before reading choices on any assumption question, negate."

> [!trap] Logging everything
> A log with thirty fields per row stops being maintained inside a week. Six fields, thirty seconds per entry, and only for misses and uncertain-but-correct answers.
>
> The log you actually keep beats the comprehensive one you abandon.

## Key takeaways

- Memory over-weights the families you dislike; a log counts what actually costs you.
- Six fields per miss, and the two that matter are why the credited answer wins and the rule to carry forward.
- Assign one of four causes: method, execution, clock, reading. Each needs a different fix.
- Review weekly for ten minutes; drill the family with the most misses.
- A rule appearing three weeks running is not being applied — attach it to a moment.
